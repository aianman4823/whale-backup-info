Privacy policy

This policy describes the Google Drive integration and backup behavior intended for Whale Backup, a private operations tool for a whale identification service. Cloud backup deployment is being validated. This policy must be reviewed before enabling the integration and kept current if its behavior changes.

Google data access and purpose

The local tool requests Google's drive.file permission. It accesses the backup folders and files it creates, or files explicitly granted to it, and their metadata. It uses this access to upload encrypted backups, list available backups, check integrity, and download data for restoration. It does not request access to unrelated Drive documents, email, contacts, or calendars.

Backup contents and storage

Backups may contain application account details, research images, labels, inference history, audit records, model and index files, and operational configuration, including application secrets required for recovery. These contents are encrypted on the operator's computer before transfer to their Google Drive.

The Google OAuth client configuration, refresh token, and backup encryption password are stored in restricted local files, separately from the uploaded backup payload. The operator must also keep a recovery copy of the encryption password in a secure place outside that computer. Local snapshot and restore files can contain decrypted data and must be protected by the operator.

Sharing and use limitations

The operator controls access to the Drive account and backup repository. The tool does not automatically share backups with other Drive users, publish their contents, sell Google user data, or use it for advertising or generalized AI or machine-learning training. Google stores and processes the encrypted backup objects as the storage provider under its own terms and privacy policy.

Whale Backup's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements.

Retention, deletion, and revocation

Automatic deletion is disabled by default. The operator selects an appropriate retention policy and removes backups they no longer need. Revoking Google access stops future authorized transfers; it does not delete backup files already stored in Drive or local copies. To remove stored data, the operator must delete the backup files and local copies, and empty the applicable trash when appropriate.

An authorized operator can revoke the app's access through their Google Account connections settings and remove the local credentials. Before deleting recovery data or keys, the operator should verify that a required recovery copy remains.

This website

These static information pages do not use analytics scripts, advertising, cookies, sign-in forms, or upload forms. The hosting provider may process ordinary request information to serve the website under its own privacy policy.

Contact and changes

For questions, use the support email shown on Google's consent screen or your existing service support channel. Material changes to Google data access or use must be reflected in this policy before the changed behavior is enabled.
